This page is for officers, leads, or anyone who isn't going to touch the code but wants to know
what the bot actually does day-to-day. No code, no technical setup — just what happens, when,
and where you'd see it.

## What it does on its own

| When this happens | The bot does this | You'd see it in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Someone joins the Discord server | Posts a welcome message mentioning them, with links to Notion, Schej, and team info | The server's welcome channel |
| A member's info changes in Notion (position, team, active status) | Updates their Discord roles to match, usually within seconds | Their roles on Discord |
| A member is marked inactive in Notion | Removes their team roles, applies an "Inactive" role, and sends them a DM explaining why | A DM to that member, and their role tag everywhere else |
| Someone clicks "Check In or Out" in the shop channel | Adds or removes them from a live list of who's currently in the shop, and logs the time | The shop check-in channel, and the attendance records in Notion |
| Someone submits the feedback form | Forwards the message to a private officers-only channel | The officers' feedback channel — nowhere else |
| A recurring meeting in Notion hits its next occurrence | Creates the next instance on the calendar automatically | The Notion calendar |

## What officers/leads can trigger manually

A handful of slash commands exist for setup and manual overrides: pointing the bot at which
channel is the check-in channel, feedback channel, or logging channel; forcing a full role
resync; checking everyone out at once (e.g. end of day); and a basic health check (`/ping`).
These require Discord permissions to run — regular members can't trigger them.

## What it does NOT do

<Callout type="info" title="Scope">
    If you're wondering whether the bot does something not listed above, the honest answer is
    probably not — it's a small, purpose-built bot, not a general moderation or automation tool.
</Callout>

- It does not read, log, or moderate regular chat messages. The only "logging" it does is
  mirroring its own internal errors to a private channel for whoever maintains it — that's about
  the bot's health, not about what members say.
- Feedback submissions are genuinely anonymous — the bot doesn't attach who submitted it when
  forwarding to officers.
- It doesn't manage roles unrelated to team membership/technical lead/inactive status — anything
  else assigned manually on Discord is left alone.
- It doesn't post anything publicly on someone's behalf without an explicit action from them
  (joining, checking in, submitting feedback) — there's no autonomous posting beyond the fixed
  messages above.
